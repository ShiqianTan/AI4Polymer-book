Cancer immunotherapy: Copycat scaffolds induce T cell expansion
Shimona Starling1
Nature Reviews Materials
volume 3, Article number: 18005 (2018) Cite this article
Save article
View saved research
980 Accesses
5 Altmetric
Metrics details
Therapeutic ex vivo T cell expansion may be induced by a new scaffold system that mimics antigen-presenting cells (APCs), report David Mooney and colleagues in Nature Biotechnology.
T cell-based therapies have shown promise for the treatment of cancer, and involve isolating T cells from a patient and manipulating them ex vivo to induce the expansion of therapeutic T cells, which are then transferred back into the patient. Effective T cell expansion requires three signals, which in vivo are provided by APCs: stimulation of the T cell receptor; stimulation of the co-receptor CD28 and the provision of the pro-survival cytokine interleukin-2.
